Please create an email notification focused on improving my fantasy football roster through the waiver wire.

Before forming this week's advice, call `GetRecommendations` to review the waiver and roster moves you advised in prior weeks, and note whether those calls have worked out. As you finalize each actionable recommendation below, record it with `AddRecommendation` (player name, direction, reason, and a confidence between 0 and 1).

### Roster Needs

* Review my roster and identify its most significant weaknesses.
* Consider injuries, underperformance, declining roles, bye weeks, positional depth, and players who may have lost or gained meaningful opportunity.
* Distinguish between problems that require an immediate roster move and weaknesses that can reasonably be addressed later.
* Consider my league's scoring settings, roster positions, bench size, and waiver-wire rules when evaluating needs.
* Prioritize weaknesses based on their expected impact on my ability to compete, not simply the positions where I have the fewest players.

### Waiver Wire Targets

For each significant roster weakness, identify relevant players currently available on the waiver wire.

* Evaluate players based on expected opportunity, role, talent, recent usage, team environment, injury situations, and rest-of-season outlook.
* Prioritize players who address an identified weakness rather than simply selecting the highest-ranked available players.
* Consider both immediate value and potential future value.
* Identify players whose value could change substantially because of an injury, depth-chart change, or emerging role.
* Do not recommend a player solely because of a single unusually productive game.
* Account for the likelihood that the player's current opportunity is sustainable.
* Consider the quality of the players I would need to drop to make room.

### Recommended Moves

Provide specific waiver-wire recommendations.

For each recommendation:

* State the player to add and the player to drop, when a drop is appropriate.
* Give one sentence or less explaining why the move addresses a specific roster need.
* Prioritize actionable moves over a long list of speculative players.
* If a waiver target is worth adding but there is no obvious player to drop, identify the weakest roster spot that could reasonably be used.
* If there are no meaningful waiver moves available, say so rather than manufacturing recommendations.

Example:

* Add Player B, drop Player A. Player B has assumed a larger workload following an injury and addresses my lack of RB depth.

### Priority Claims

If the league uses waiver claims:

* Identify the players worth prioritizing with waiver claims.
* Consider the likelihood that each player retains his role, expected rest-of-season value, and the opportunity cost of using a higher waiver priority.
* Do not assume a player should be prioritized simply because he had the highest fantasy score the previous week.

### Players to Monitor

Include a short section identifying players who are not necessarily waiver additions yet but whose roles or usage should be monitored over the next 1–2 weeks.

First review any stored memories for players or situations you flagged in prior weeks, and note whether those situations have progressed, resolved, or should continue to be monitored. If the current watch list or any rest-of-season strategy is worth carrying into future runs, persist it with a memory.

Focus on:

* Emerging workloads
* Increasing snap or route participation
* Changes in target or carry share
* Injuries creating potential opportunities
* Players whose recent production may be misleading

Use the latest available information as of the time the email is generated. Prioritize current-week news and developments from the previous 24–48 hours, while using season-long data to establish context.
